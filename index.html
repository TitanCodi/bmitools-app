Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about BMI, calories, body fat, and healthy weight.

BMI Calculator

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered normal (healthy weight) for adults aged 20 and older, according to the World Health Organization. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above falls into the obese range. These thresholds apply equally to men and women.

The healthy BMI range for women is the same as for men: 18.5 to 24.9. However, women naturally carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, so body fat percentage provides a more complete picture for women.

BMI is a useful screening tool but has well-known limitations. It can overestimate body fat in muscular athletes (who may register as overweight despite low body fat), and underestimate it in older adults who have lost muscle mass. It also doesn't account for fat distribution. Use BMI alongside waist circumference and body fat percentage for a fuller picture.

BMI is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by the square of your height in meters: BMI = kg ÷ m². In imperial units: BMI = (lbs ÷ in²) × 703. For example, a person who weighs 165 lbs and stands 5'10" has a BMI of approximately 23.7 — right in the healthy range.

For adults 20 and older, the same BMI thresholds apply regardless of age. However, older adults tend to have more body fat at the same BMI than younger adults. For children and teens (ages 2–19), BMI is assessed using age- and sex-specific growth percentile charts.

Daily Calories & TDEE

To lose approximately 1 lb (0.45 kg) per week, eat roughly 500 calories below your TDEE per day. For 2 lbs/week, cut 1,000 calories. Most health professionals recommend not going below 1,200 calories/day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision. Use the TDEE calculator above to find your personal baseline.

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total calories your body burns in a day, including your basal metabolic rate, physical activity, and digestion. It's the most important number for weight management: eat below it to lose, at it to maintain, and above it to gain. A sedentary and a very active person of the same size can differ by 700–1,000+ calories per day.

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain vital functions. TDEE multiplies BMR by an activity factor to estimate total daily calorie burn. Think of BMR as your engine idling and TDEE as your total fuel use for the day.

Your maintenance calories equal your TDEE. For most moderately active adults this is 1,800–2,500 cal/day for women and 2,200–3,000 cal/day for men, though individual variation is significant. The TDEE calculator above uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most validated formula for estimating energy needs.

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation used here is accurate to within ±10% for most people and is considered the most reliable formula by major nutrition organizations. The biggest source of error is the activity multiplier — most people underestimate how sedentary they are. When in doubt, choose a lower activity level and adjust based on real-world results over 2–3 weeks.

Body Fat & Ideal Weight

Healthy ranges differ by gender. For men: athletic 6–13%, fit 14–17%, average 18–24%, obese 25%+. For women: athletic 14–20%, fit 21–24%, average 25–31%, obese 32%+. Women naturally require more essential fat due to hormonal and reproductive functions. These ranges are from the American Council on Exercise.

From most to least accurate: DEXA scan (gold standard, requires clinic), hydrostatic weighing, BodPod, skinfold calipers (professional), bioelectrical impedance (home scales, highly variable). The Navy tape measure method in our calculator is free and within ~3–4% accuracy for most people.

Yes — this is called normal weight obesity or "skinny fat." A person can have a BMI of 18.5–24.9 but carry disproportionately high body fat due to low muscle mass. This is one of the key reasons body fat percentage is a more informative health metric than BMI alone, particularly for sedentary individuals and older adults.

There is no single ideal weight — it depends on your height, frame size, muscle mass, and gender. As a quick reference: for a 5'10" male, common formulas suggest roughly 166–172 lbs; for a 5'6" female, roughly 130–140 lbs. Use the Ideal Weight Calculator above for personalized estimates across the Hamwi, Devine, and Robinson formulas.

Healthy weight range is a broad span (30–40 lbs for most heights) derived from BMI thresholds of 18.5–24.9. Ideal weight is a more precise point estimate from clinical formulas accounting for gender and frame size. Ideal weight typically falls near the middle of the healthy weight range.

Water Intake

The "8 glasses a day" guideline is a rough estimate. A more personalized approach is roughly 35 ml per kg of body weight, adjusted for activity and climate. For a 165 lb (75 kg) moderately active person, this equals about 90–100 oz (2.6–3 liters) per day. About 20% of daily water comes from food, so your drinking target is somewhat less. Use the Water Intake Calculator above for your number.

Research suggests adequate hydration supports weight management: drinking water before meals can reduce calorie intake, cold water slightly boosts metabolism, and staying hydrated reduces fatigue that can be misread as hunger. Water is not a weight loss solution on its own — it works best as part of a calorie-controlled diet and active lifestyle.

Yes. A general guideline is an extra 12–16 oz (350–500 ml) per 30 minutes of moderate exercise. For intense exercise over 60 minutes, electrolyte replacement also becomes important. Hot or humid climates increase needs further. The Water Intake Calculator applies an activity multiplier automatically.

All beverages count toward hydration — water, coffee, tea, juice, and milk all contribute. Even foods with high water content (cucumbers, watermelon, soup) count. However, plain water is the best choice because it has no calories or additives. Caffeinated drinks have a mild diuretic effect but still net-positive for hydration at normal consumption levels.
